## You can code. Nobody taught you production.

You are in your final year of a CS degree, fresh out of a bootcamp, or a year or two into your
first job. You can write Python or JavaScript, you know roughly what an HTTP request is, and you
have created a database table or two. Then someone says "put a rate limiter in front of it" or
"make that endpoint idempotent", and everyone nods except you. Or your team wants to add search
over its documents with an AI model, and words like embeddings, RAG and evals fly past.

Every page here is written the way a patient senior engineer would explain it at your desk, in
two parts. Part 1 explains the whole idea in plain words, following one example with real numbers,
so that after ten minutes or so you can explain it to a friend. Part 2 goes deep: what people try
first and why it breaks, how it really works, and how real systems do it. No assumed background
beyond the basics, every term explained before it is used, and every calculation written out.

## How to read a topic page

Every topic follows the same shape, so after the first one you always know where you are. If you
are short of time, read Part 1: it ends with a summary, and it is enough to understand the idea. If a
page lists topics under **Read first**, those are the ideas it builds on. Skim them if any feel
unfamiliar; the page still makes sense without them, but it makes more sense with them.
